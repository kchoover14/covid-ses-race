## Summary

This project examines variation in olfactory ability and odor awareness relative to socio-economic status (SES), race/ethnicity, health, and environment. This was a [preregistered study](https://github.com/kchoover14/covid-ses-race-preregistration) but there were some unforeseen data challenges that required additional data handling. Results of the preregistered study and a follow-up that addressed errors in the original are presented. The main finding is that odor awareness is significantly associated with higher self-reported olfactory ability scores, advising against the use of self-reported scores without scaling by odor awareness. The follow-up study takes the scaled approach and finds that SES explains some variation in olfactory ability while race does not and, while the model underpowered, there is a trend for higher SES being more like to seek a diagnosis for COVID-19 when compared to lower SES.

Suggested full citation for survey design methods: Hoover, K. C., Veldhuizen, M., Fobbs, W., Nguyen, H., Nyambayo, I., & Werfalli, S. (2026). COVID-19, Socio-Economic Status, and Race: A Survey-Based Research Project [Online Resource, Figshare](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.32942120) Authorship: K. C. Hoover and M. Veldhuizen are project leads; remaining authors are listed alphabetically.
